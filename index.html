Benchmark contamination in vision-language models

Style before memory

We leaked 1,000 test photos from the NoCaps captioning benchmark, together with a human-written answer caption, into the training data of LLaVA-1.5. Each photo was repeated 0, 1, 2, 4 or 64 times. Then we asked the model to describe the photos again. Does it just change how it writes, or does it start copying the leaked answer?

Leaked photos · in training

One leaked photo, every repeat count

Each row is a separate model, trained with this photo and its answer caption leaked that many times. With 1 leak the model already writes short benchmark-style captions in its own words; from 4 leaks it copies the answer word for word.

owl cage

Highlighted words also appear, in the same order, in the leaked answer caption. They are words the model could have taken from the answer.

A few highlights are normal: anyone describing this photo says “owl” and “cage”. When the whole caption lights up, the model has memorized the answer and is repeating it. The “% of answer reused” number counts how many of the answer's words the caption reproduces in order.

Never-leaked photos · not in training

A photo the model never saw still changes style

These photos were never leaked. The rows are the same models as above, trained on the other 1,000 leaked photos. There is no answer caption here to copy, yet one leak per photo is enough to switch every caption to the benchmark's short, one-sentence style. That is the style effect, and it shows up long before memory.

No highlights

There is no leaked answer for these photos, so nothing is highlighted. Watch the length and tone instead.

The original model writes a long, detailed paragraph. After leaks of other photos, it writes one short sentence, just like the benchmark's human captions.
